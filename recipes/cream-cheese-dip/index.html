Cream Cheese Dip

A no-cook dip: 500g of cream cheese beaten smooth with the juice of a lemon, capers and 250g of smoked salmon folded through, chilled for an hour, then left out for a few minutes so it scoops. Smoke, salt and lemon against a thick dairy base. Fifteen minutes of work and about four cups, enough for sixteen to twenty people with crackers or bagels. The notebook page carries four ingredients and no method at all, so the steps below are drafted from the published versions of the same dip and held to his four ingredients.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20262 min read

  • Prep 15 min
  • Makes About 4 cups, serves 16 to 20

Before you cook this.

This dip is never cooked, and cold-smoked salmon is not cooked either. Cold smoking runs below about 30C, which cures and flavours the fish without killing listeria, and refrigeration slows listeria rather than stopping it. Pregnant people, adults 65 and over, and anyone immunocompromised should use cooked or hot-smoked salmon here instead of cold-smoked; heating cold-smoked salmon into a cooked dish is the other safe route. Hot-smoked salmon is cooked to 63C / 145F and carries none of this.

A dairy and fish dip with no cooking step. Keep it refrigerated, return it to the fridge within two hours, and eat it within three days. Fish and dairy are both major allergens, and mayonnaise brings egg if you add it as a loosener.

Ingredients

  • 1 500g pack cream cheese
  • 5 teaspoons capers
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 250 g of cold-smoked salmon (lox)

Equipment. Mixing bowl, Hand mixer or food processor

Method

  1. Bring the cream cheese to room temperature
  2. Chop the capers finely
  3. Flake or finely chop the salmon
  4. Beat the cream cheese with most of the lemon juice until smooth
  5. Fold in the salmon and capers
  6. Taste and add the remaining lemon juice a little at a time until it balances
  7. Chill for an hour before serving
  8. Let it sit out for a few minutes before serving so it scoops

Can you eat this if you are pregnant

Not as written, and the reason is worth a sentence because it runs against the habit most people have been taught. Cold-smoked salmon is cured and smoked below about 30C, which never cooks it, so listeria present in the fish is not killed. Refrigeration is the control that works on almost everything else and it does not work on listeria, which grows at fridge temperature.

Pregnant people, adults 65 and over, and anyone immunocompromised should use cooked or hot-smoked salmon in this dip rather than cold-smoked. Hot-smoked salmon is cooked to 63C / 145F during production and carries none of this. The other safe route is to heat cold-smoked salmon into a cooked dish, which is not what this dip is.

For everyone else the dip is fine as written. Keep it refrigerated, get it back in the fridge within two hours, and eat it inside three days.

Will it actually scoop

Half a kilo of cream cheese loosened by nothing but one lemon will not. Every published version of this dip adds a loosener, either sour cream or mayonnaise, at about a quarter cup per 8 ounces of cream cheese. Scaled to this batch that is roughly half a cup of each, or a cup of one.

Bringing the cream cheese fully to room temperature first, and letting the chilled dip stand for a few minutes before it goes on the table, are the two steps that do the most for texture without changing his ingredient list.
