Blueberry Muffins

Creamed-butter blueberry muffins made with a full cup of plain yogurt, which is what keeps them soft for a second day. The method is the standard one: cream, add eggs, add the wet, fold in the dry and the frozen berries, then 25 to 30 minutes at 350F. About 20 minutes of work. Read the note on the butter before you start, because the notebook calls for 1.5 cups and that is roughly three times what a muffin of this size normally takes.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

What goes into it

  • 1.5 cups of butter (room temp)
  • 1 cup of sugar
  • 2 eggs (room temp)
  • 1 teaspoon of vanilla extract
  • 1 cup plain yogurt
  • 1/4 cup of milk
  • 2.5 cups of flour
  • 2 teaspoons of baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon of baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon of salt
  • 1/2 to 1 cup of frozen blueberries

Equipment. 12-hole muffin tin, Paper liners

How to make it

  1. Preheat the oven to 350F
  2. Cream the butter and sugar
  3. Add eggs one at a time
  4. Add vanilla, yogurt and milk, mix together
  5. In a separate bowl, combine dry ingredients
  6. Combine all ingredients and blueberries
  7. Bake for 25-30 mins

Is 1.5 cups of butter right?

It is what the notebook says. I went back to the photograph and read the line at magnification to be sure, and it holds: one and a half cups.

It is also a long way outside what a muffin takes. At 1.5 cups the butter weighs about 340g against 313g of flour, so there is more fat than flour in the bowl, with two eggs asked to emulsify all of it. That is a pound cake ratio, or a shortbread one. Baked as written you should expect something dense and rich and close-grained rather than a light muffin crumb.

For this flour weight most recipes use about 1/2 cup of butter. Three quarters of a cup is the outside edge if you want it genuinely rich. Everything else on the page lines up almost exactly with the reference recipe once you scale it: sugar at 1 cup against a scaled 1.07, milk at 1/4 cup against a scaled 0.36, eggs at 2 against a scaled 2.9. One ingredient sitting at two or three times the expected figure while the rest match is what makes the butter stand out.

The recipe is his and it stays as he wrote it. If you want a muffin rather than a small cake, use 1/2 cup and leave the rest alone.

What goes into it

A full cup of plain yogurt is generous, about 40 per cent more than the reference at this flour weight, and it is doing the work that buttermilk does elsewhere. It also argues that the fat was meant to be modest, because the two together would be very heavy.

The sugar is all white. Swapping a quarter cup of it for brown sugar gives a moister chew if that is what you are after.

The berries are on the low side. Half a cup reads sparse across 12 or more muffins, and the top of that range, a full cup, is closer to what you would want.

How to get a good crumb

Stop mixing the moment the flour disappears. Overmixing is the usual cause of a tough muffin with tunnels through it, and it is easy to do when the batter looks lumpy and you keep going.

Let the eggs, yogurt and milk sit out until they are no longer fridge-cold. Cold liquid hitting creamed butter splits the batter.

Toss the frozen blueberries in a spoonful of the measured flour before folding them in, or they sink to the bottom and bleed through the crumb. Fill the liners right to the top for a domed muffin instead of a flat one.

Where this version differs

The butter reads 1.5 cups in the notebook and I have kept it. It is roughly three times the usual figure for 2.5 cups of flour, which most recipes put at about 1/2 cup, with 3/4 cup as the ceiling. At 1.5 cups the butter outweighs the flour, 340g to 313g, with only two eggs to hold it, which gives a pound cake rather than a muffin.

The blueberries are low against the reference, which scales to about 2 cups at this flour weight. Use the top of his range, a full cup, or more.

The reference bakes 5 minutes at 425F before dropping to 350F, to force a taller dome. His straight 350F bake gives a flatter muffin, which is a difference in the result rather than a fault.
