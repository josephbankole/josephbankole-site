Fried Chicken Bao

Steamed bao buns filled with corn flour fried chicken and a gochujang glaze. Three parts on one notebook page: a yeasted dough that rests for 90 minutes and steams for 12, chicken thighs cut small, salted, dredged and fried at 350F, and a sauce of soy, sugar, cider vinegar, gochujang, ketchup and garlic simmered down for five minutes. Allow about three hours from start to plate, nearly all of it dough rising, so do not plan dinner around the 12 minute steam. The chicken has to reach 74C / 165F. His dough runs wetter than published versions, which the notes cover.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20264 min read

  • Prep 2h 30m
  • Cook 40 min
  • Makes 5 to 6 bao on these quantities; serves 2 to 3

Before you cook this.

Chicken is done at 74C / 165F in the thickest part. The notebook page gives no frying time, no oil temperature and no doneness cue for the chicken at all, so the steps above supply them: 350F oil, 5 to 6 minutes for bite-size pieces, thermometer to confirm. Whole thighs take longer and have to be checked rather than guessed. Above about 375F the corn flour browns before the thigh is cooked, which is the failure that matters because it looks done.

Do not fill the pot more than a third with oil, and fry in batches, because crowding drops the temperature hard. Pat the chicken dry and lower it in gently. If the oil catches fire, cut the heat and cover the pot; never bring water near it. The sauce is sugary and catches fast, so keep it at a simmer and stir it.

Ingredients

  • Bao: 1.5 cups flour
  • Bao: 1 tsp baking powder
  • Bao: 1 tsp yeast
  • Bao: 1/3 cup sugar
  • Bao: 1/3 cup oil
  • Bao: 3/4 cup warm water
  • Bao: 4 tsp milk
  • Chicken: 4 thighs
  • Chicken: 2 tsp sea salt
  • Chicken: 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • Chicken: 1 cup corn flour
  • Chicken: neutral oil for deep frying, roughly 1 litre (not in the original notebook, the Chicken component lists no fat at all, and the 1/3 cup of oil on the page belongs to the bao dough and is used up there)
  • Sauce: 1/3 cup soy sauce
  • Sauce: 1/3 cup sugar or honey
  • Sauce: 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar
  • Sauce: 2 tbsp gochujang
  • Sauce: 2 tbsp ketchup
  • Sauce: 3 cloves garlic

Equipment. Steamer basket, Squares of baking paper to sit the buns on, A deep, heavy pot for frying, Instant-read thermometer

Method

  1. Bao: Mix dry ingredients, make a well in the middle
  2. Bao: Mix wet ingredients, then stir with dry
  3. Bao: Knead for 5 minutes
  4. Bao: Cover and rest for 90 minutes
  5. Bao: Once risen, knead for 5 minutes
  6. Bao: Cut dough into circles
  7. Bao: Fold each circle and use oil on the inside to avoid sticking
  8. Bao: Steam for 12 minutes
  9. Chicken: Cut the thighs into bite-size pieces
  10. Chicken: Season all over with the sea salt and black pepper
  11. Chicken: Leave for 20 minutes until the surface looks wet
  12. Chicken: Dredge each piece in corn flour and press the coating on
  13. Chicken: Shake off the loose flour
  14. Chicken: Heat oil to 350F
  15. Chicken: Fry in batches for 5 to 6 minutes until golden and cooked through
  16. Chicken: Rest on a wire rack
  17. Chicken: For extra crunch, fry a second time for 1 to 2 minutes
  18. Sauce: Mince the garlic
  19. Sauce: Put the soy sauce, sugar or honey, vinegar, gochujang, ketchup and garlic in a small pan
  20. Sauce: Bring to the boil, then simmer for 5 minutes until it coats a spoon
  21. Sauce: Toss the fried chicken in the sauce while both are still hot
  22. Sauce: Fill the steamed bao straight away

How long does it really take

About three hours, and the 12 minute steam at the end is the smallest part of it. The 90 minute rest is the bulk, and published versions add a further 30 minute prove after shaping that his page does not carry.

Active work is around half an hour: mixing and kneading, cutting and folding the circles, twenty minutes of salted chicken sitting while you get the oil up, then roughly fifteen minutes of frying in batches and five reducing the sauce. Toss the chicken in the sauce hot and fill the buns straight away, because a glazed piece sitting on a rack goes soft.

Why does the dough look so wet?

Because on these numbers it is. One and a half cups of flour is about 190 g, against 3/4 cup of water at roughly 180 g, plus 1/3 cup of oil and 1/3 cup of sugar. The published version this page tracks uses about 360 g of flour against the same 180 g of water, so his flour is close to half.

The page photograph was checked again rather than assumed. One and a half cups is genuinely what he wrote, and so is the 4 tsp of milk, so both stay. If the dough will not come together firmly enough to knead and cut into circles, flour is the thing to add, a spoonful at a time.

What to put in the bun

The page has no vegetable and no acid anywhere in it, which leaves a bun of fried chicken in a sugar and soy glaze with nothing cutting it. Published versions treat sliced cucumber, spring onion, fresh coriander and sesame seeds as part of the dish rather than a garnish, and a squeeze of lime does the same job.

Sit each bun on a square of baking paper in the steamer. Without one they weld to the basket and tear when you lift them.
