Sausage Rolls

Ground sausage seasoned with black pepper, garlic, paprika and thyme, mixed with softened onion, rolled up in puff pastry and baked at 400F until the pastry is golden. About 25 minutes of work and 20 to 30 minutes in the oven depending on how thick you roll them. They come out savoury and peppery, with a crisp shell over a dense, fatty filling. Six hundred grams of sausage and one 450g pack of pastry gives roughly 24 pieces, enough for eight to ten people as party food. Give them five minutes out of the oven before anyone bites into them.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

The notebook writes '1 pack of PM puff pastry'. PM is President's Choice, sold in 450g packs across the Loblaws shops, so I have written it as plain puff pastry with the weight. Any 450g pack does the same job.

Before you cook this.

The notebook's 20 minutes at 400F is fine for pieces of 1.5 to 2 inches and short for anything thicker. A 5 inch log needs 30 to 35 minutes. Sausage meat is ground pork and is done at 71C / 160F; UK guidance uses the stricter 75C / 167F. Probe the centre of the meat rather than judging by the colour of the pastry, which browns long before the middle arrives.

What goes into it

  • 600g / 1.3 pounds of ground sausage
  • 2 medium eggs
  • 1/2 of an onion
  • 1 pack of puff pastry, 450g / 1 lb, thawed
  • 2 tablespoons of flour
  • 1 teaspoon each of black pepper, garlic, paprika, thyme

Equipment. Baking tray, Instant-read thermometer

How to make it

  1. Cook the onions on medium for 7 minutes
  2. Mix together in a large bowl: sausage, cooked onions, eggs (1, maybe 2)
  3. Preheat the oven to 400F
  4. Cut the pastry into 2 long strips
  5. Add the sausage filling to the middle of the pastry rectangle
  6. Brush the edges with egg wash
  7. Fold the pastry over to make long rolls
  8. Brush with egg wash and cut into pieces of 1.5 to 2 inches
  9. Bake for 20 minutes. Anything left as a 5 inch log needs 30 to 35 minutes instead
  10. Probe the centre of the meat, not the pastry. Sausage meat is done at 71C / 160F

What goes into it

Six hundred grams of ground sausage, half an onion softened in a pan for seven minutes first, and a teaspoon each of black pepper, garlic, paprika and thyme. The onion going in cooked rather than raw is a good instinct and neither reference recipe bothers with it.

The pastry is a single 450g pack, cut into two long strips. That loads about 300g of sausage into each strip, which is a meat-to-pastry ratio almost exactly in line with the published versions, though it makes for a fat roll.

How big should the pieces be

The notebook says 20 minutes at 400F and never says how big the finished pieces are. That missing dimension is the defect, not the time. Twenty minutes gets small pieces of 1.5 to 2 inches, cut from a thin roll, up to temperature. Full-size 5 inch logs take 30 to 35 minutes in every published version.

There is a second lever. Cutting the pastry into three strips instead of two gives thinner rolls, and thinner rolls come up to temperature inside the twenty minutes he wrote. That is the easier fix if you want to keep his clock.

Either way, probe the centre of the meat. Puff pastry goes golden and convincing well before a thick core of sausage reaches 71C / 160F, so colour tells you nothing useful here.

Where this version differs

Step 2 says 'eggs (1, maybe 2)', which is the notebook hesitating. Both reference recipes put no egg in the filling at all and use their eggs as wash. Sausage meat is fatty enough to bind itself, and an egg-bound filling firms up towards meatloaf. One egg for the wash is the safe reading; put a second in the filling only if you want that firmer set.

The seasoning line has no salt in it, and the 2 tablespoons of flour never appear in any step. The flour is presumably for dusting the work surface. If you want a binder for the rendering fat, breadcrumbs are what the published recipes reach for, about half a cup.

Where this version differs

He loads about 300g of sausage into each of two strips, roughly double the meat per roll of either reference recipe. Cutting the pastry into 3 strips instead of 2 gives thinner rolls and lets the original 20 minutes stand.

Consensus puts zero eggs in the sausage filling. Both references use theirs entirely as egg wash. His own step 2 reads 'eggs (1, maybe 2)', so the uncertainty is his, and one egg for the wash is the settled answer.

'PM puff pastry' is President's Choice Butter Puff Pastry, a 450g pack sold across the Loblaws banners. Published here as a 450g pack of puff pastry.
