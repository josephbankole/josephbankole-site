Tartar Sauce

A cup of mayonnaise stirred through with finely diced dill pickles and shallot, chopped capers, a tablespoon of pickle juice and the juice and zest of a whole lemon. Ten minutes of chopping, then an hour in the fridge so the flavours settle. It makes about a cup and a half, which is eight to twelve servings at two tablespoons, and it keeps up to two weeks in a sealed container. Sharper than anything from a jar, because of the pickle juice and the zest. It belongs with the beer battered fish written on the same notebook page.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20261 min read

  • Prep 10 min
  • Makes About 1.5 cups, 8 to 12 servings

Before you cook this.

No cooking and no raw protein, so there is nothing to check for doneness. It is mayonnaise based: keep it refrigerated, and use it within two weeks in an airtight container.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup of mayonnaise
  • 2 dill pickles, finely diced
  • 1 shallot, finely diced
  • 1 tablespoon of capers, finely chopped
  • 1 tablespoon of pickle juice
  • 1 lemon (juice and zest)

Equipment. Zester or fine grater, Airtight container

Method

  1. Combine the ingredients for the tartar sauce
  2. Cover and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes, an hour is better, so the flavours marry. Taste and season after the rest, since the salt reads differently once it settles

Does it need to rest

Yes, and this is the one thing the notebook leaves out. The James Beard version says outright that the sauce should stand about an hour and gains a great deal as the flavours merge and mellow, and every other published version chills it before serving. Thirty minutes is the floor.

Season after the rest rather than before. Pickles, capers and pickle juice all carry salt, and how much of it reads in the finished sauce changes once everything has sat together.

What to serve it with

The beer battered fish it was written beside, which has its own page here. Make the sauce first so it can rest while the fish is cut, salted and battered.

It keeps up to two weeks in an airtight container in the fridge. It is mayonnaise based, so keep it cold at the table rather than letting the bowl drift warm next to a tray of hot fish.
