Birria Ramen

Birria ramen is a birria broth used as a ramen broth. Chuck roast, bone-in short ribs and oxtail simmer for three hours with a spice sachet, then dried chillies and white miso go into the blender with two cups of the liquid and back into the pot, and the beef is shredded through it. The miso is what pushes it towards ramen rather than tacos. It makes about three quarts, which is six bowls with noodles. Total time is roughly four hours and most of that is the pot doing the work on its own.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

  • Prep 35 min
  • Cook 3h 20m
  • Makes 6

Before you cook this.

Step 12 blends two cups of near-boiling broth on high. Vent the blender lid or let the liquid cool for a few minutes first, or the steam lifts the lid and the contents go up the wall.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb / 450 grams of boneless chuck roast
  • 3 large short ribs with bone
  • 1 lb / 450 grams of oxtail
  • 1 tbsp of oxtail seasoning
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp black peppercorns
  • 1 tbsp coriander seeds
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 4 green onions plus 1 onion
  • 6 cloves garlic
  • 1 inch knob ginger
  • Cilantro root
  • 3 qts / 2.8 L beef stock (1 cube for 4 cups)
  • 5 guajillo chillis, 5 ancho chillis and 2 chili d'arbol, OR 1 tbsp of gochujang
  • 3/4 cup of white miso
  • Oil for browning (not in the original notebook, step 4 says to add oil but no cooking fat appears in his list)
  • 6 portions of ramen noodles, about 600 g fresh or 6 packets of instant with the seasoning sachets thrown away (not in the original notebook, the dish is named for them and they appear nowhere in his list or steps; 6 portions matches his 3 qts of stock)

Equipment. Cheese cloth, Large heavy pot with a lid, Blender

Method

  1. Brown the meat for 3-5 mins per side
  2. Season with oxtail seasoning (pat the meat dry first)
  3. Add cinnamon, bay leaves etc to cheese cloth and seal securely
  4. Set heat to medium and add oil
  5. Add tomato paste, cook for 1 min
  6. Add green onions, garlic, ginger, beef, sachet and stock
  7. Bring to a boil on med-high then immediately reduce to a simmer
  8. Add the chillies (whole)
  9. Cover and simmer for 3 hours
  10. Remove beef and put in a bowl
  11. Add chillies (or gochujang), 2 cups broth, 3/4 cup of miso
  12. Blend on high till smooth
  13. Season to taste with salt and pepper
  14. Shred the beef in a bowl

Should you toast the chillies first

Yes, and my steps do not. The chillies go in whole and untoasted at step eight and get blended at step eleven with no rehydrating in between.

Toasting them 30 to 60 seconds a side in a dry pan before they go anywhere pulls the smoke and the fruit out of a guajillo. Strain the blended mixture as it goes back into the pot as well. Chilli skins do not break down, and a bowl that is supposed to be silky ends up gritty.

How to build the bowl

My steps end at shredding the beef, which leaves the last five minutes undocumented. Cook the noodles separately in boiling water, two to three minutes for fresh ramen, and drain them. Instant will soften in about five minutes under hot broth if you would rather not run a second pot.

Noodles into the bowl, shredded beef on top, broth ladled over. Diced white onion, coriander and a lime wedge are the standard garnishes, and a halved soft-boiled egg if you want it to look like ramen as well as taste like it. The red chilli oil that floats to the top of the broth is a feature, so do not skim it all off.

Store leftovers with the noodles separate from the broth. Left sitting in it overnight they turn to paste.

Is gochujang a fair swap for the dried chillies

No. My ingredient list offers one tablespoon of gochujang as an alternative to the guajillo, ancho and arbol, and that is not a like-for-like exchange.

Sixty to eighty grams of dried Mexican chilli carries the colour, the body and the whole fruit-and-smoke backbone of the broth, and the blended skins thicken it as well. Fifteen grams of gochujang is sweet, soy-funky and thin. It makes a different bowl, which is fine as long as nobody is calling it birria.

If you want the pantry route to work, use three to four tablespoons of gochujang with two tablespoons of sweet paprika for the colour, and cut the miso from three quarters of a cup to a half so the bowl is not fermented and salty twice over.
