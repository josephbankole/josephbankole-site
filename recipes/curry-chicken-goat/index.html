Curry Chicken or Goat

Curry chicken or goat, Trinidadian style, where the curry powder is slaked with hot water and burned in hot oil before the meat goes anywhere near it. That step is what separates a Trini curry from an Indian or a Jamaican one. The meat is rinsed with lime and vinegar, seasoned with garlic, onion and green seasoning for half an hour, then cooked down with potatoes and carrots until tender. Three pounds serves six to eight. Chicken is done in about forty-five minutes; goat wants two to two and a half hours of slow braising, so choose the protein by how much of the day you have.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20262 min read

  • Prep 45 min
  • Cook 45 min
  • Makes 6 to 8

Before you cook this.

Chicken is done at 74C / 165F in the thickest part.

Ingredients

  • 3 lbs of chicken or goat
  • 1 teaspoon of minced garlic
  • 2 tablespoons of green seasoning
  • 1 teaspoon of salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon pepper sauce
  • 3-4 tablespoons curry powder
  • 1/2 cup of chopped onions
  • 1/2 cup of hot water
  • 2 medium potatoes, cubed (not in the original notebook, named in step 9 but absent from his list; 2 potatoes is the standard quantity at this meat weight)
  • 1 to 2 carrots, sliced (not in the original notebook, named in step 9 but absent from his list; carrots are his own addition, most Trini curry uses potato alone)
  • Lime and vinegar, for rinsing the meat (not in the original notebook, step 2 rinses with both and neither is in his list)
  • Oil for the pan (not in the original notebook, step 4 heats oil and there is none in his list)

Method

  1. Cut chicken or goat into small pieces
  2. Rinse with lime and vinegar
  3. Season with garlic, onion and green seasoning, leave for 30 minutes
  4. Heat oil in a pan
  5. Cook remaining onions and garlic till fragrant
  6. Mix curry powder with hot water
  7. Add curry mix to hot oil
  8. Add chicken or goat and cook until the water dries out
  9. Add chopped potatoes and carrots
  10. Add some water if needed
  11. Cook until tender

Why the curry powder is mixed with water first

Curry powder is slaked with hot water into a paste, and the paste goes into hot oil and is cooked hard before the meat arrives. Trini cooks call it chunkay and treat it as the step you do not skip. It takes the raw edge off the spice and gives the finished pot the toasted, geera-forward taste that separates this from other curries.

The notebook's sequence here is more orthodox than several of the popular recipes I compared it against, which do the same thing without naming it. Do not reorder these steps.

How long does it really take

Two different answers for one recipe, which is the notebook's one real omission. Chicken is tender in about forty-five minutes from the moment the meat goes in. Goat is a collagen-heavy cut and needs an hour and a half to two and a half hours of wet braising, or roughly forty-five minutes under pressure.

A reader who applies the chicken clock to goat gets something chewy and a pot that has run too dry for too long. Check the liquid every half hour on the long version.
