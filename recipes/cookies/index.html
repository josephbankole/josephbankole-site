Cookies

Chocolate chip cookies built on a cup of butter to a cup and a half of flour, with a chopped chocolate bar instead of chips and a teaspoon of cornstarch for softness. The scooped dough is frozen before it goes in, which is what stops a mixture this rich from spreading flat. Twenty minutes of mixing, at least half an hour in the freezer, then 8 to 10 minutes at 425F. Makes about 18 medium cookies, thick in the middle with edges that have set.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20262 min read

What goes into it

  • 1 cup of butter (room temp)
  • 1/2 cup of brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup of white sugar
  • 1 egg (room temp)
  • 1 teaspoon of vanilla
  • 1.5 cups of flour
  • 1 teaspoon of corn starch
  • 1/2 teaspoon of salt
  • 1 teaspoon of baking soda
  • 1 bar of chocolate, 100g to 200g

Equipment. Baking sheets

How to make it

  1. Cream the butter and sugar
  2. Add the egg and vanilla and mix until smooth
  3. Mix dry ingredients in another bowl
  4. Chop the chocolate bar into small pieces
  5. Combine all ingredients together
  6. Scoop out individual cookies and freeze
  7. Bake at 425 for 8-10 mins

What goes into it

A cup of butter against a cup and a half of flour is a lot of fat, roughly double what most published chocolate chip cookies carry, and one egg is holding all of it together. The freezing step is what makes that work, so it is not optional here.

The chocolate is a bar chopped by hand rather than chips. Chips hold their shape by design; chopped bar gives you shards and pools and a scatter of dust through the dough. My notebook says Cadbury, but any plain bar of 100g to 200g does the same job.

The teaspoon of cornstarch is a softness trick and it matches what most current recipes do. There is no baking powder, only soda, which gives a flatter and chewier cookie than a soda-and-powder pair would.

Why the oven is at 425F

425F sits about 50F above the usual band for chocolate chip cookies, and it is right for this dough. Very high fat, dough balls frozen solid, very hot oven: that combination is the method Levain-style bakeries use, where the heat sets the outside before the middle has time to spread.

The number was struck through and rewritten as 425 in the notebook, which reads like a correction made after a bake rather than a slip of the pen.

How to stop the bottoms burning

At 425F the bases set two or three minutes before the centres do, so 8 to 10 minutes will darken the undersides of a normal-size cookie. Lift one at 8 minutes and look underneath before committing the tray.

Pull them while the edges are set and the middles still look wet. They finish on the hot sheet, so give them 5 minutes there before moving them.

Freeze the scooped dough for at least 30 minutes, and space the balls a good 2 inches apart. This much butter spreads.

Where this version differs

425F is well above the 350F to 375F that the top-ranking recipes use, and it stays. It is the hot-oven, frozen-dough method that a 0.67 fat-to-flour ratio needs. The bake time is the part I would watch: 8 to 10 minutes is the correct window at 375F, and at 425F it risks a dark base. Check the underside at 8 minutes.

The notebook says one bar of Cadbury chocolate. Bars run 100g to 200g, so the quantity is a range rather than a figure. I have written it as a plain chocolate bar with that weight range.
