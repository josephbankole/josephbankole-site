Pancakes

Plain American pancakes from seven ingredients, no buttermilk and nothing to soak overnight. The batter is mixed until the flour streaks disappear and no further, rested for five minutes, then cooked a quarter cup at a time on a lightly greased pan. Ten minutes of preparation and about fifteen at the stove. Makes 8 or 9 pancakes, thick and light because of an unusually heavy dose of baking powder, which is intentional and explained below.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20262 min read

  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 15 min
  • Makes 8 to 9 pancakes

Ingredients

  • 1.5 cups of flour
  • 3.5 teaspoons of baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon of salt
  • 1 tablespoon of white sugar
  • 1.25 cups of milk
  • 1 egg
  • 3 tablespoons of melted butter
  • A little butter or neutral oil for the pan (not in the original notebook, his 3 tablespoons of melted butter all go into the batter, leaving nothing to grease the pan; without it the first pancake sticks)

Equipment. Frying pan or griddle

Method

  1. Whisk the flour, baking powder, salt and sugar in a large bowl
  2. Make a well in the middle
  3. Whisk the milk, egg and melted butter together in a second bowl
  4. Pour the wet mix into the well and stir just until the streaks of flour disappear
  5. Leave the batter to rest for 5 minutes
  6. Heat a pan over medium for about 2 minutes
  7. Grease the pan lightly and wipe most of it off
  8. Pour about 1/4 cup of batter per pancake
  9. Cook until bubbles form and pop on the surface, 1 to 2 minutes
  10. Flip and cook until golden, 1 to 2 minutes
  11. Re-grease every two or three pancakes

Is 3.5 teaspoons of baking powder a mistake?

No. It is what the source recipe calls for and it is deliberate.

The rule of thumb is 1 to 1.25 teaspoons of baking powder per cup of flour, so 3.5 teaspoons across a cup and a half is more than double that. It is also the figure in one of the most-cooked pancake recipes on the internet, and the bakers who have doubled it to seven teaspoons say the same thing: it is not a typo, the extra lift is the whole point of these pancakes being tall.

Anyone who knows the ratio will assume the notebook slipped. It did not.

Is a teaspoon of salt too much?

It is faithful to the source, and it is the most-argued-over number in that recipe's review history. A teaspoon of table salt to a cup and a half of flour is two to four times what comparable recipes use, and readers regularly report the result as too salty and halve it.

Half a teaspoon is the safe figure if you have not made these before. The notebook's teaspoon stays on the page because that is what it says.

How to get them light

Stop stirring while the batter is still lumpy. Lumps cook out; a smooth, beaten batter goes rubbery.

Rest it for five minutes before the first pancake. That short pause is the single biggest lever on how fluffy they come out, because it lets the flour hydrate and the baking powder start working.

Flip when the bubbles on the surface are bursting and staying open, not at the first sign of one. Grease the pan again every couple of pancakes.
