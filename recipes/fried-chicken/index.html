Fried Chicken

Southern fried chicken with a gochujang note in the wet dredge. Dry-brine the thighs with kosher salt, pat them dry, then take them twice through gochujang milk and twice through seasoned flour. The double dredge builds the craggy crust. Two to four thighs feeds two to four people. Allow at least half an hour for the brine and about 25 minutes of frying.

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 25 min
  • Makes 2 to 4

Ingredients

  • 2-4 boneless chicken thighs
  • 1 tablespoon of kosher salt
  • 1.5 cups of milk
  • 1.5 cups of flour
  • 1 teaspoon of gochujang
  • 2 teaspoons of black pepper
  • 2 teaspoons of garlic powder
  • 2 teaspoons of paprika
  • 1 teaspoon of cayenne pepper
  • 1 teaspoon of onion powder
  • Oil for deep frying (not in the original notebook, step 6 heats oil to temperature but no frying oil appears in his list)

Equipment. Heavy deep pot or Dutch oven, Instant-read thermometer, Wire rack over a sheet pan

Method

  1. Sprinkle kosher salt all over chicken
  2. Let it brine in the refrigerator until the chicken is sweating, at least 30 minutes
  3. Pat dry the chicken
  4. Mix gochujang and milk together
  5. Mix flour and seasoning together
  6. Heat oil to 350
  7. Dunk chicken in milk, then flour, then milk, then flour again
  8. Fry for 8 to 10 minutes, until the thickest part reads 74C / 165F

How long do you fry it?

Bone-in thighs want 325F for 12 to 15 minutes, turned every three or four minutes. The bone is a heat sink and the meat next to it heats last.

Boneless thighs need much less. They will be done in about five minutes at 375F.

Chicken is done at 74C / 165F in the thickest part. On dark meat, 168F to 175F eats better, because thigh connective tissue is still tough at 165F.

Fry two to four pieces at a time and no more. A crowded pot drops the oil below 300F and the crust goes greasy instead of crisp.

How do you keep the crust on?

Rest the dredged pieces on a rack for ten to fifteen minutes before they go into the oil. The flour hydrates and grips.

Drain the cooked chicken on a wire rack over a sheet pan, not on paper towel. Paper steams the underside and undoes the crust.

Buttermilk instead of plain milk helps here, and so does a quarter cup of cornstarch in the flour.

What do you serve it with?

Hot sauce, pickles and white bread. Coleslaw and cornbread if it is a meal rather than a snack. It is better at room temperature an hour out of the fryer than reheated.
