Fried Chicken

American Southern fried chicken with a gochujang note in the wet dredge. The thighs are dry-brined with kosher salt, patted dry, then taken twice through gochujang-spiked milk and twice through seasoned flour before they go in the oil. The double dredge is what builds the craggy crust. The seasoning is the standard Southern set of black pepper, garlic powder, paprika, cayenne and onion powder, with the gochujang sitting underneath rather than on top. Two to four thighs feeds two to four people. Allow at least half an hour for the brine and about 25 minutes of frying in batches.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 25 min
  • Makes 2 to 4

Before you cook this.

Chicken is done at 74C / 165F in the thickest part. These are boneless thighs, and at that size 350F for 8 to 10 minutes gets there, which is what my notebook says. Bone-in thighs are a different job and need roughly 325F for 12 to 15 minutes, so do not use these times for them.

Brine the chicken in the refrigerator, not on the counter. Throw the gochujang milk away once the chicken has come out of it; it has held raw poultry and cannot be served as a sauce.

Ingredients

  • 2-4 boneless chicken thighs
  • 1 tablespoon of kosher salt
  • 1.5 cups of milk
  • 1.5 cups of flour
  • 1 teaspoon of gochujang
  • 2 teaspoons of black pepper
  • 2 teaspoons of garlic powder
  • 2 teaspoons of paprika
  • 1 teaspoon of cayenne pepper
  • 1 teaspoon of onion powder
  • Oil for deep frying (not in the original notebook, step 6 heats oil to temperature but no frying oil appears in his list)

Equipment. Heavy deep pot or Dutch oven, Instant-read thermometer, Wire rack over a sheet pan

Method

  1. Sprinkle kosher salt all over chicken
  2. Let it brine in the refrigerator until the chicken is sweating, at least 30 minutes
  3. Pat dry the chicken
  4. Mix gochujang and milk together
  5. Mix flour and seasoning together
  6. Heat oil to 350
  7. Dunk chicken in milk, then flour, then milk, then flour again
  8. Fry for 8 to 10 minutes, until the thickest part reads 74C / 165F

How long to fry it and how to know it is done

This page assumes bone-in thighs, which is what most people have when a recipe says 'chicken thighs'. Bone-in wants 325F for 12 to 15 minutes, turned every three or four minutes. The bone is a heat sink and the meat next to it is the last part of the piece to come up to temperature.

My notebook said 350F for 8 to 10 minutes. That schedule suits boneless thighs, which need much less time; boneless will be done in about five minutes at 375F. On a bone-in piece it is short, which is why the numbers on this page differ from the ones in the book.

Chicken is done at 74C / 165F in the thickest part. On dark meat, 168F to 175F eats better, because thigh connective tissue is still tough at 165F. That second number is texture, not safety, and the two are doing different jobs.

Fry two to four pieces at a time and no more. Cold chicken pulls the oil temperature down, and a crowded pot drops it below 300F, at which point the crust goes greasy instead of crisp.

How to keep the crust on

Rest the dredged pieces on a rack for ten to fifteen minutes before they go into the oil. The flour hydrates and grips, and skipping this is the usual reason a coating slides off in the fryer.

Drain the cooked chicken on a wire rack over a sheet pan rather than on paper towel. Paper steams the underside and undoes the crust you just spent fifteen minutes building.

Buttermilk instead of plain milk would help here, and so would a quarter cup of cornstarch in the flour. Neither is in my notebook and both are in almost everybody else's.

What to serve it with

Hot sauce, pickles and white bread if you are being traditional about it. Coleslaw and cornbread if it is a meal rather than a snack. It is better an hour out of the fryer at room temperature than it is reheated.
