Coconut Milk Shrimp

A pound of shrimp seared, set aside, and returned at the end to a pan sauce of coconut milk, stock, garlic, fresno chilli, paprika, sugar and fish sauce, thickened with a corn starch slurry and finished with cilantro. Sweet, savoury and mildly hot, and it wants rice underneath it. About fifteen minutes of preparation and fifteen at the stove, serving four. The sequence is right, in particular returning the shrimp off the boil rather than simmering them in the sauce. The coconut milk and stock quantities are worth reading before you shop, and there is a note on them below.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

The page is named for the coconut milk and then gives three times as much stock as coconut milk. That looks like a slip of the pen, and it stays as he wrote it with a note rather than being quietly swapped.

Before you cook this.

Shrimp are done at 63C / 145F, opaque and curled into a loose C. The timings on this page overshoot rather than undershoot, so the risk here is rubbery shrimp and not raw ones.

What goes into it

  • 1 lb of shrimp
  • 3 tablespoons of oil
  • 1 tablespoon of minced garlic
  • 1 tablespoon of fresno chili
  • 1 tablespoon of paprika
  • 3/4 cup of stock
  • 1/4 cup of coconut milk
  • 1.5 tablespoons of sugar
  • 1 tablespoon of fish sauce
  • 1/2 tablespoon of corn starch
  • 1/2 tablespoon of water
  • 2 tablespoons of chopped cilantro
  • Salt and black pepper, for seasoning the shrimp (not in the original notebook, both are named in step 1 but neither is in his list)

How to make it

  1. Season shrimp with salt and pepper
  2. Cook shrimp for 4 minutes
  3. Remove shrimp
  4. Fry garlic, chili and paprika
  5. Add stock, coconut milk, sugar, fish sauce and bring to simmer
  6. Add corn starch slurry
  7. Add shrimp and cilantro

What goes into it

A pound of shrimp, three tablespoons of oil, and an aromatic base of minced garlic, fresno chilli and a full tablespoon of paprika. The sauce is stock and coconut milk with sugar and fish sauce, thickened with a corn starch slurry, and two tablespoons of chopped cilantro go in at the end with the shrimp.

Salt and pepper are named in the first step and missing from the list, so they are promoted above. Two things to watch in the pan: pat the shrimp dry before they go in or they steam instead of colouring, and give the paprika thirty seconds and no more before the liquid arrives, because it scorches fast and turns bitter.

How much coconut milk should go in?

As written, a quarter cup, against three quarters of a cup of stock. In a dish named after the coconut milk that is very likely the two figures the wrong way round. The reference version uses a whole 14 oz can of coconut milk on the same pound of shrimp and no stock at all, and nothing in the wider set runs stock-dominant.

The corn starch slurry is the tell. It is needed precisely because three quarters of the liquid is thin stock. Flip the two to 3/4 cup coconut milk and 1/4 cup stock and the sauce thickens on its own, the slurry becomes optional, and it tastes like its name. His numbers stay on the page as he wrote them, so this is your call to make.

What to serve it with

Rice. A cup of pan sauce with nothing under it is half a dish, and the sauce here is built to be soaked up.

Four minutes is long for shrimp that then go back into a hot sauce. A pound is cooked through in two to three minutes in total, so pull them at a minute or two a side, deliberately underdone, and let the return trip finish them. There is no lime anywhere on the page either, as with the other Thai recipes in the notebook, and a wedge on the side does a lot here.

Where this version differs

A quarter cup of coconut milk against three quarters of a cup of stock is almost certainly the two quantities reversed. The reference uses a full 14 oz can of coconut milk and no stock on the same pound of shrimp. His figures are kept as written and flagged here rather than corrected on the page.

Four minutes for the first cook is longer than shrimp want, given they return to the sauce at the end. Published versions run one to two minutes a side. The sequence itself is right, and returning the shrimp off the boil rather than simmering them in the sauce is the move that matters most.

No lime and no shallot or scallion. Published versions finish with the juice and zest of a whole lime and build the base on a shallot as well as garlic. Coconut milk, sugar, fish sauce and paprika with no acid at all reads flat, and the lime is the omission a cook notices on the plate.
