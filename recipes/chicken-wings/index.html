Chicken Wings

Two kilos of wings marinated in light and dark soy, oyster sauce and cooking wine, dredged in a flour and corn starch coat, then double fried. Savoury and salty rather than hot, with a thin brittle crust that comes off the corn starch. The timing is the part to read. The schedule written in the notebook gives a flat about four minutes of frying in total and a drum about seven, and four minutes will not bring a bone-in wing from fridge temperature to a safe 74C / 165F. The schedule on this page is a corroborated one instead. Allow about 50 minutes.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 30 min
  • Makes 6 to 8

Before you cook this.

THE ORIGINAL TIMING WAS SHORT OF A SAFE INTERNAL TEMPERATURE. The notebook reads "Fry at 300F: 5 mins for drums, 2 mins for flats", then "Fry at 350F for 2 min". That gives a flat about four minutes of frying in total and a drum about seven. Four minutes will not bring a bone-in wing from refrigerator temperature to 74C / 165F, and seven sits at the very bottom of any published range. Two independent cooking references confirm it. The steps on this page use America's Test Kitchen's corroborated schedule instead: 350F for about 7 minutes, rest 5 minutes, then 375F for about 7 minutes. USDA FSIS supplies the temperature: all poultry to 165F, measured with a thermometer, with colour and clear juices explicitly not reliable indicators.

Chicken is done at 74C / 165F in the thickest part. Wings eat better at 79C / 175F, which is a texture judgement and not a safety one: a wing sits between white meat and dark, and the connective tissue needs the extra heat to break down. ThermoWorks fry at 350F rather than the more usual 375F for exactly that reason, so the skin does not overcook before the meat gets there. Two different numbers for two different reasons, and they should not be blurred together.

A batch of cold wings pulls the oil temperature down hard, and 300F is a low first fry before that happens. For much of the original two minute flat fry the oil would have been sitting under 300F. A flat is also bone-in, so the meat next to the bone is the last part to come up and the bone itself acts as a heat sink. The thin piece is not the safe piece.

Ingredients

  • 2 kg of chicken wings
  • 1/2 cup of flour
  • 1/2 cup of corn starch
  • 1.5 teaspoons of salt
  • 3 teaspoons of sugar
  • 1.5 teaspoons of white pepper
  • 3 tablespoons of light soy sauce
  • 3 tablespoons of oyster sauce
  • 3 tablespoons of cooking wine
  • 1.5 tablespoons of dark soy sauce
  • Neutral oil for deep frying (not in the original notebook, steps 8 and 9 fry the wings at a stated temperature but no oil appears in his list)

Equipment. A deep, heavy pot or a fryer, Instant-read thermometer, Wire rack

Method

  1. Wash and cut wings
  2. Mix together marinade
  3. Marinate chicken
  4. In a bowl mix flour, corn starch and seasoning
  5. Pat dry the chicken wings
  6. Roll the wings in flour to coat
  7. Squeeze the wing to incorporate
  8. Fry at 350F for about 7 minutes, in batches of roughly 1.5 lbs so the oil holds its temperature
  9. Rest the wings for 5 minutes
  10. Fry again at 375F for about 7 minutes
  11. Check the thickest wing reads at least 74C / 165F before serving. 79C / 175F eats better on a wing

How long do they actually need in the oil?

About fourteen minutes of frying in total, split across two goes with a rest between: 350F for around seven minutes, five minutes off the heat, then 375F for around seven more. That is America's Test Kitchen's schedule and it is the one used here. ThermoWorks give a single fry alternative at 350F for seven to ten minutes, also verified with a thermometer.

The clock is not the load-bearing instruction, the thermometer is. Batch size, how cold the wings were, and how fast the oil recovers all move the time. Two kilos is a lot of wings for a domestic pot, so work in batches of about 1.5 lbs and let the oil climb back between them.
