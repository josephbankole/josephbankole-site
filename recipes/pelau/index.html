Pelau

Pelau is Trinidadian one pot rice, and the step that defines it is the burnt sugar. Browning is caramelised in hot oil until dark, seasoned chicken goes in to take the colour, then rice, chopped vegetables, coconut milk and broth, and the pot simmers covered until the liquid has gone. It eats sweet and savoury at once, with a faint smokiness off the sugar. This version has no pigeon peas, which most recipes treat as the thing that makes pelau pelau. Marinate ahead, and it is about forty minutes at the stove for four servings.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

Before you cook this.

Chicken is done at 74C / 165F in the thickest part.

What goes into it

  • 1.5 lbs of chicken
  • 1/2 teaspoon of salt
  • 1 tablespoon of green seasoning
  • 1 teaspoon of minced garlic
  • 1/2 teaspoon of worcestershire and soy sauce
  • 1/2 teaspoon of ketchup
  • 1.5 tablespoons of browning
  • 1 cup of rice
  • 1/2 cup of chopped onion and veggies
  • 1/2 tablespoon of salt
  • 1 cup of coconut milk
  • 1 cup of broth
  • Oil for the pot (not in the original notebook, step 2 heats oil and there is none in his list)

How to make it

  1. Season the chicken and let marinade
  2. Heat oil and cook browning for 2 minutes
  3. Add chicken and cook for 5 minutes
  4. Add rice and cook for 3 more minutes
  5. Add chopped veggies and cook for 2 minutes
  6. Add salt, coconut milk and broth
  7. Cover and simmer for 20 minutes

What goes into it

A pound and a half of chicken, seasoned with green seasoning, garlic, worcestershire, soy and a little ketchup, then a cup of rice cooked in a cup of coconut milk and a cup of broth. The browning is the engine: a tablespoon and a half of it, caramelised in the oil for two minutes before anything else goes in.

There is no pigeon pea in the list and none in the steps. Thyme and a whole uncut scotch bonnet are standard elsewhere and absent here too, though the green seasoning covers a good deal of what the thyme would have done.

Why salt appears twice

The list carries half a teaspoon of salt and half a tablespoon of salt, and both belong. The first seasons the chicken at the marinade stage in step 1. The second seasons the rice and the liquid in step 6. It reads like a duplication and it is not, so do not drop one. Total comes to about two teaspoons against a pound and a half of chicken, a cup of rice and two cups of liquid, which is on the light side of most published pelau.

Where this version differs

Pigeon peas. Every source I checked treats them as constitutive rather than optional, to the point that two of them put the peas in the title. At this scale that would be about three quarters of a cup cooked, or half a fifteen ounce can, drained, going in with the rice. Without them this is a coconut chicken rice on a burnt sugar base, which is a good thing to eat and is not quite pelau.

The covered simmer is written as a flat twenty minutes. A cup of rice in two cups of liquid can want twenty-five, so go by the liquid rather than the clock, and give the pot ten minutes covered off the heat before forking it through.

Where this version differs

No pigeon peas. Every Trinidadian source compared treats pigeon peas as constitutive of pelau; at this scale that is about 3/4 cup cooked, or half a 15 oz can, drained. The notebook version is kept as written.

The two salt entries, 1/2 teaspoon and 1/2 tablespoon, are correct as written. They are two separate additions, one for the chicken and one for the rice.

Marinating time is not stated in the notebook. Published Trini pelau asks for 4 to 5 hours minimum, overnight preferred.

The covered simmer is written as 20 minutes. Consensus is 25 to 30, judged by the liquid being absorbed rather than by the clock.
