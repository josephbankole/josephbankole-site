Beer Battered Fish

Two pounds of cod or haddock cut into pieces and salted, dipped in a batter of flour, garlic powder, cayenne, white pepper, old bay, one egg and a can of ale, then fried at 365F for 3 to 5 minutes a batch. The crust is spiced and the fish stays soft under it. It serves four to six and runs to about 20 minutes of preparation plus 20 at the pan across three or four batches. The tartar sauce shares this page in the notebook and is made at the same time, so mix that first and let it sit while the oil comes up.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

This and the tartar sauce are written on one notebook page under a single numbered list. The first step, 'combine ingredients for tartar sauce', belongs to the sauce; the remaining four are all fish, in sequence. I have split them into two pages, but they are one job.

Before you cook this.

Fish is done at 63C / 145F, or when it turns opaque and flakes cleanly. At 365F for 3 to 5 minutes on cut pieces that is met comfortably; the notebook simply gives no cue, so this is a gap in the writing rather than a short cooking time. On the oil: keep the pot no more than half full, pat the fish dry before it goes near the batter, and lower each piece in away from you. Two pounds of wet fish going into 365F oil is the real hazard on this page.

What goes into it

  • 2 lbs of cod or haddock
  • 1 tablespoon of kosher salt
  • 1.25 cups of flour
  • 1 tablespoon of garlic powder
  • 2 teaspoons of cayenne, white pepper, old bay
  • 1 egg
  • 12 ounce can of ale or blonde beer
  • Neutral oil for deep frying (not in the original notebook, step 4 fries at 365F but no oil, pot or depth is listed)

Equipment. Deep pot or fryer, Frying thermometer, Wire rack

How to make it

  1. Cut the fish into pieces and salt them
  2. Mix the dry ingredients for the batter, then add the beer
  3. Flour the fish before dipping
  4. Fry for 3 to 5 minutes at 365F, in batches. The fish is done at 63C / 145F, when it turns opaque and flakes cleanly

What goes into it

Two pounds of cod or haddock, salted with a tablespoon of kosher salt before anything else touches it. The batter is 1.25 cups of flour carrying a tablespoon of garlic powder and two teaspoons each of cayenne, white pepper and old bay, then one egg and a 12 ounce can of ale or blonde beer.

The dry flour dredge in step 3, before the fish goes into the wet batter, is what gives the batter something to grip. Skipping it is how batter slides off in the oil.

What to serve it with

The tartar sauce, which is written on the same notebook page and has its own page here. It wants an hour in the fridge, so it goes together first and rests while the fish is cut, salted and battered.

Chips and mushy peas are the obvious rest of the plate. The sauce is mayonnaise based, so keep the bowl cold rather than letting it sit beside a tray of hot fish all evening.

Where this version differs

The batter is flour, egg and beer with no leavener. Every crisp beer batter I checked relies on baking powder, self-raising flour or the carbonation of a fresh can to aerate. Scaled to 1.25 cups of flour that is about 1.5 teaspoons of baking powder. Without one, the crust fries dense rather than puffed, and that is the change a cook would feel immediately.

The reference batter also runs nearly half its flour weight as rice flour, which keeps the crust crisp as it cools. Swapping a quarter to a third of a cup of the flour for cornstarch or rice flour closes most of that gap.

The frying figures need no correction. 365F sits squarely in the usual 350F to 375F band, and 3 to 5 minutes on cut pieces is at the short end of the 4 to 6 minute consensus but inside it.

Where this version differs

No baking powder, no self-raising flour and no carbonation allowance. This is the material omission. About 1.5 teaspoons of baking powder scaled to his 1.25 cups of flour is what the published batters use to aerate the crust.

The reference batter substitutes rice flour for close to half its flour weight to hold the crust crisp as it cools. A quarter to a third of a cup of cornstarch or rice flour in place of the same amount of flour does the same job here.

His 365F and 3 to 5 minutes hold up against the published figures. Mix the batter just before frying, leave it lumpy, keep the beer and the fish cold, and fry in batches so the oil does not crash.

The tartar sauce on the same notebook page is published separately here. They were written as one job and are best made as one.
