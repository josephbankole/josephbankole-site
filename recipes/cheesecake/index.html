Cheesecake

A no-bake cheesecake on a cookie base, set with sweetened condensed milk and a quarter cup of lemon juice rather than gelatin. The acid firms the milk proteins and the cold does the rest, so it slices cleanly without ever seeing an oven. Twenty minutes of work, then a minimum of four hours in the fridge and preferably overnight. It is sweet and sharp at once, and the lemon here is double what most versions use. Serves 8 to 10.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

Before you cook this.

No eggs and no cooking, so there is no pathogen risk in the method. It is still a fresh dairy dessert: keep it in the fridge and eat it within three or four days.

What goes into it

  • 1 cookie pie base
  • 2 tablespoons of sugar
  • 2 8-ounce packs of cream cheese (room temp)
  • 1 14-ounce sweetened condensed milk
  • 1/4 cup lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon of vanilla extract
  • or 1.25 cups of whipped cream in place of the condensed milk
  • 5 to 6 tablespoons of melted butter, if you are making the base from scratch (not in the original notebook, step 1 makes the base with melted butter but it is absent from his ingredient list)

Equipment. 9 inch pie dish or springform tin

How to make it

  1. Make cookie base with melted butter
  2. Beat cream cheese and condensed milk or cream
  3. Add lemon juice and vanilla
  4. Refrigerate overnight

Does it set without gelatin?

Yes, and reliably. The acid in the lemon juice reacts with the concentrated milk proteins in the sweetened condensed milk, and the cold firms the fat. Two 8 ounce packs of cream cheese to one 14 ounce tin of condensed milk is the classic ratio and needs nothing added.

A quarter cup of lemon juice is double what the reference uses, which means more acid and a firmer, sharper set rather than a looser one.

Four hours in the fridge is the floor. Overnight is better and is what the notebook says.

What happens if you use whipped cream instead

The alternative on the page swaps the condensed milk for a cup and a quarter of whipped cream. As written it will not set. Beating liquid cream into cream cheese gives an aerated mixture that slumps instead of slicing, because the acid and milk solids that were doing the setting have gone and nothing has replaced them.

To make it work, whip the cream separately to stiff peaks and fold it into the beaten cream cheese rather than beating them together. Add half to three quarters of a cup of icing sugar, since the condensed milk was carrying all the filling's sugar. Keep the lemon juice for both flavour and firmness.

Even done properly it is a softer, mousse-textured cheesecake and it wants a full overnight chill. The condensed milk version is the one I would make.

How to keep the filling smooth

Beat the cream cheese on its own first, three or four minutes, until there is no lump left in it. Every grain you can still see at that point will still be there at the end.

It has to be at room temperature and it has to be full-fat block cream cheese. The whipped and spreadable tubs carry too much water and will not set.

For the base, about a cup and a half of cookie crumbs to 5 or 6 tablespoons of melted butter, with the 2 tablespoons of sugar. Press it in and chill it while you make the filling. If the cookie base is a bought one, skip the butter entirely.

Where this version differs

The whipped cream alternative does not set as the notebook writes it. The cream has to be whipped to stiff peaks and folded in, not beaten in with the cream cheese, and the filling needs 1/2 to 3/4 cup of icing sugar to replace the sugar the condensed milk was carrying.

It is not clear from the page whether 1.25 cups of whipped cream means liquid cream, which whips to about 2.5 cups, or that volume already whipped, which is about 2/3 cup of liquid cream.

The melted butter appears in the method with no quantity and is missing from the ingredient list, and the page does not say whether the cookie base is bought or made.

No-bake, so there is no cook time. The setting happens overnight in the fridge rather than in an oven.
