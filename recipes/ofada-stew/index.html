Ofada Stew

Ofada stew is a Nigerian pepper stew built on bell peppers and scotch bonnets, blended raw, squeezed dry and then fried down until the water is gone and the flavour concentrates. My version uses beef shanks, dried crayfish and a cup of beef stock, and it runs about an hour and forty minutes from the first rinse to the last simmer. It comes out hot and thick rather than soupy, and it goes with rice, ofada rice if you can get it. There is no palm oil and no iru in what I wrote down, which is a real departure from the standard version.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

Two things most Nigerian cooks would call essential are absent from my notebook: palm oil and iru. I have kept the recipe as I wrote it and put the quantities you would need in the notes, so you can put them back if you want to.

What goes into it

  • 1.5 lbs beef shanks
  • 4 assorted bell peppers
  • 3 scotch bonnet peppers
  • 1 onion
  • 1 tablespoon of dried crayfish
  • 1 cup of beef stock
  • 1 tablespoon of paprika, salt, pepper

Equipment. Blender, Cheese cloth, Oven-safe pan

How to make it

  1. Rinse beef with lime and vinegar
  2. Cook beef on medium for about 20 mins, season
  3. Transfer to the oven at 350 for 20 mins
  4. Blend peppers, onion, paprika and seasoning
  5. Squeeze out the water with cheese cloth
  6. Fry some onions
  7. Fry pepper mix for about 20 mins
  8. Add dried crayfish
  9. Fry on low-medium for 20 mins
  10. Add stock and beef
  11. Cook on low-medium for 20 mins

What goes into it

The pepper base is four assorted bell peppers, three scotch bonnets and an onion, blended raw with paprika and seasoning. Four bells to three bonnets is a hot mix. Drop to one or two bonnets if you are cooking for people who are not used to it, and keep the bells at four so the volume of the sauce does not change.

Blending peppers raw releases a lot of water, and water is the enemy of this stew. I squeeze the blend through cheese cloth before it goes anywhere near the pan. Most recipes get to the same place by frying the water off over twenty or thirty minutes. The cloth is faster and it gives you a drier, more concentrated base to start from.

The dried crayfish is doing the savoury work. A tablespoon sounds like nothing in a pot this size, but it is a strong ingredient and it carries.

Where this version differs

My ingredient list carries no cooking fat of any kind, and steps six and seven both say to fry. Every version of this dish I can find uses palm oil, and uses it as the point rather than as a medium. It is where the colour comes from, and most of the aroma. At my meat weight, about half a cup is the standard dose.

The other absence is iru, fermented locust beans. Two tablespoons is typical. I have the dried crayfish, which is one half of that flavour pair, and not the iru, which is the other. Without both, this is closer to a green pepper stew than to ayamase.

If you do add palm oil you have a decision to make about bleaching it, meaning heating it past its smoke point until it goes pale. Some traditional methods insist on it and some newer ones refuse. Bleaching fills a kitchen with dense smoke very quickly, so if you go that way, open everything first.

How long does the beef really need

Beef shank is one of the most collagen-heavy cuts on the animal, and collagen only gives up under long wet heat. My schedule gives it twenty minutes on the hob, twenty in the oven and twenty in the sauce, which is about an hour in total and mostly dry.

Recipes that get shank properly tender simmer it for 45 to 90 minutes before it ever meets the sauce. If you want it falling apart rather than chewy, simmer the shanks first and then carry on with my step two, or leave the final simmer running for 45 to 60 minutes instead of 20.

What to serve it with

Ofada rice, which is a short unpolished local rice with a strong smell that people either love or do not. Plain boiled long grain works and is what I usually have. Boiled eggs on top are traditional and I have left them out.

Where this version differs

My list has no palm oil and no iru. Those two are most of what makes a stew ofada rather than a green pepper stew. At my meat weight of 1.5 lbs the standard quantities are about 1/2 cup of palm oil and 2 tablespoons of iru (fermented locust beans). Add them if you want the traditional version.

My beef gets about an hour in total, and only the last 20 minutes of it is wet. Consensus is 45 to 90 minutes of wet cooking before the meat goes into the sauce. Beef shank will still be chewy at my timing. Simmer it first, or run the final simmer for 45 to 60 minutes.
