Barbacoa

Barbacoa is beef braised slowly with dried chillies until it shreds under a fork. Mine is two pounds of brisket and two of beef cheeks, cut into three-inch cubes, seared and then covered with beef stock, whole guajillo and ancho chillies, garlic, bay, oregano, cumin and a cinnamon stick. It goes into a low oven for four to six hours and comes out as pulled beef sitting in a dark, mildly spiced broth. Four pounds of meat feeds eight to ten. It is a taco filling first and a bowl of beef and broth second.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20262 min read

  • Prep 30 min
  • Cook 5h
  • Makes 8 to 10

Before you cook this.

My notebook gives no oven temperature. This page uses 300F, covered, for 4 to 6 hours. The defensible range is 275F to 325F, where 325F lands nearer 4 hours and 275F needs the full 6. Do not go by the clock alone: the beef is ready when a fork shreds it with no resistance, which is roughly 203F in the middle.

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs of beef brisket
  • 2 lbs of beef cheeks
  • Salt to taste
  • 2 cups of beef stock
  • 8 cloves of garlic
  • 4 guajillo chiles
  • 3 ancho peppers
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 2 teaspoons of oregano and cumin
  • 1/4 cup of water
  • 1 cinnamon stick, about 3 inches (not in the original notebook, called for in step 4 but absent from his list; Mexican canela if you can get it)

Equipment. Dutch oven or heavy lidded braiser, Parchment paper

Method

  1. Cut the beef into 3 inch cubes, season with salt
  2. Sear the beef for 3-4 minutes
  3. Turn off the heat
  4. Add the stock, garlic, whole chiles, cinnamon stick, bay leaf and oregano and cumin
  5. Add a layer of parchment paper on top
  6. Cover and put in the oven at 300F for 4 to 6 hours, until a fork shreds the beef with no resistance, roughly 203F inside

How long does it need in the oven

300F, covered, for four to six hours. The defensible range is 275F to 325F, and the two ends of it map onto the two ends of the time window: at 325F expect it done nearer four hours, at 275F it will want the full six.

The clock is the estimate and the fork is the answer. It is ready when a fork goes in and pulls the meat apart with no resistance at all, which lands at roughly 203F inside. If there is any spring left, it needs longer, and another forty minutes will not hurt it.

Should you toast the chillies first

Yes, and I do not. My chillies go into the pot whole and untoasted, which costs real flavour and gives a thinner, paler broth than it should be.

The better route takes about twenty minutes: toast them 30 to 60 seconds a side in a dry pan until they smell nutty, soak them in hot water for 15 to 20 minutes, then blend them with a little of the soaking liquid and strain them into the pot. Straining matters as much as toasting, because the skins do not break down and leave grit in the finished broth.

What to do with it when it comes out

Lift the meat out, shred it with two forks and skim the fat off the liquid, or leave the fat if you are dipping tacos in it. Put the meat back into the liquid for twenty minutes so it drinks some of it up, and taste for salt at that point rather than earlier.

Tacos with white onion and coriander is the default. It also keeps well: cool it within two hours and store the meat in its liquid, not drained, or it dries out in the fridge.
