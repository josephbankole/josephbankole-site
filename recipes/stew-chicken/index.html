Stew Chicken

Trinidad stew chicken is built on caramelised brown sugar. The sugar is cooked in hot oil until it goes dark, seasoned chicken goes in and takes on the colour, and the pot cooks down until the liquid dries before the broth and vegetables go back in. What comes out is dark and glossy, savoury rather than sweet, with a gravy thick enough to sit on rice. Three and a half pounds of cubed chicken serves six or seven. Once the marinating is done it is about forty-five minutes on the stove, and the only step that catches people out is the first one.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20261 min read

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 45 min
  • Makes 6 to 7

Before you cook this.

Chicken is done at 74C / 165F in the thickest part.

If the caramelising sugar blackens or starts to smoke, throw it away and start again rather than adding the chicken. Burnt sugar turns the whole pot bitter and cannot be corrected later.

Ingredients

  • 3.5 lbs of chicken (cubed)
  • 3 tablespoons of green seasoning
  • 1 teaspoon minced garlic
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon of oil
  • 3 tablespoons of brown sugar / browning
  • 1 cup of broth
  • 1 teaspoon of pepper sauce
  • 1/2 cup of chopped onion
  • 1/2 cup of chopped carrots and bell peppers

Method

  1. Season chicken and let marinate
  2. Cook brown sugar / browning in hot oil
  3. Add chicken and allow to brown
  4. Cook until the water dries up
  5. Add veggies, pepper and broth
  6. Cook on medium until tender

How to get the browning right

This is the step that goes wrong. The sugar goes into hot oil and darkens, and when it reaches deep mahogany you have roughly thirty seconds to get the chicken in. Too early and the stew is sweet. Too late and the sugar blackens, at which point the whole pot will be bitter and there is no saving it; tip it out, wash the pan and start the sugar again.

Once the chicken is in, it takes the colour rather than the sweetness. Let the water it throws off cook away before the broth goes in, or the gravy never gets there.

How long it takes

The notebook gives no times at all. Reckon on five minutes for the sugar and the sear, ten minutes cooking the liquid down, then about twenty minutes covered and a final ten uncovered to bring the gravy to the consistency you want. Forty-five minutes at the stove, on top of two hours to overnight in the marinade.
