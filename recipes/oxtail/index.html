Oxtail

Oxtail braised until it comes off the bone, on a base of caramelised brown sugar, green seasoning and browning. Two and a half pounds is rinsed with lime and vinegar, marinated overnight, browned hard, then simmered on low for three hours with beef broth. Lima beans and a squirt of ketchup go in for the last half hour, and it goes to the table with rice and plantain. Reckon on close to four hours of cooking for four to five servings, nearly all of it unattended. Best started on a Sunday morning.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20262 min read

  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 3h 45m
  • Makes 4 to 5

Ingredients

  • 2.5 lbs of oxtail
  • 1/4 cup of brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup of green seasoning
  • 1/4 cup of oxtail seasoning
  • 1 tablespoon of soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon of worcestershire sauce
  • 1/4 cup of chopped onions, garlic, ginger, pepper
  • 1 teaspoon of browning
  • 1 cup of beef broth
  • 1 can of lima beans
  • 2 tablespoons of ketchup (not in the original notebook, step 7 says a squirt; 2 tablespoons is what Trini sources use at this weight of meat)
  • Lime and vinegar, for rinsing the oxtail (not in the original notebook, step 1 rinses with both and neither is in his list)
  • Oil for the pot (not in the original notebook, browning and frying the aromatics both need it and there is none in his list)

Method

  1. Rinse oxtail with lime and vinegar
  2. Season oxtail and let marinate all night
  3. Brown oxtail on medium high
  4. Remove oxtail from pan
  5. Cook onion mix until fragrant, add broth
  6. Add oxtail and cook on low for 3 hours
  7. Add beans and a squirt of ketchup
  8. Cook another 30 mins on low
  9. Serve with rice and plantain

Is this Trinidadian or Jamaican

Both traditions cook oxtail and neither invented it. The clean dividing line the sources agree on is the sugar: Trinidadian oxtail is built on a caramelised burnt sugar base, the same step used for stew chicken and pelau, while Jamaican oxtail braises without it, taking its colour from bottled browning and soy and its signature from whole allspice berries.

A quarter cup of brown sugar caramelised at the start and a quarter cup of green seasoning put this firmly on the Trini side. The lima beans are the butter beans of the Jamaican canon, and bottled oxtail seasoning is a Jamaican retail product. It is a Trini method with two Jamaican borrowings, which seems worth saying out loud rather than picking a flag and hoping nobody notices.

How long it needs on the heat

Three hours on low, then another thirty minutes once the beans and the ketchup go in. That is at the generous end of what is published, which is the right end to be at for oxtail, and no change is worth making to it.

Two things the notebook leaves out. Brown the meat in batches rather than all at once, because two and a half pounds crowded into a domestic pot steams instead of searing and you lose the fond the gravy is built on. And look in on the liquid across those three hours; a pot that long on the heat can go dry, and older oxtail takes longer still.
