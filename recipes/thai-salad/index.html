Thai Salad

Chicken thighs cooked in green seasoning, chopped small and tossed through lettuce, cilantro, shredded carrot, cucumber and roasted peanuts, with sweet chilli sauce as the dressing. It is a shortcut version. There is no lime and no fish sauce, so the dressing reads sweet rather than sharp, and at half a cup for two people it is generous. Two thighs and a bag of salad make a main course for two or a side for four. About half an hour, most of it cooking and then cooling the chicken. Chicken is done at 74C / 165F in the thickest part.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20262 min read

Before you cook this.

Chicken is done at 74C / 165F in the thickest part. The page gives no cooking time and no doneness cue for the one raw-protein step it has. Two thighs pan-cooked run about 12 to 15 minutes, but the thermometer is the check rather than the clock.

Once it is dressed and has cooked chicken through it, this is on the usual two hour clock at room temperature. Refrigerate anything that is not eaten, and do not dress it and hold it.

What goes into it

  • 2 seasoned chicken thighs
  • 2 servings of lettuce or salad mix
  • 1 tablespoon of chopped cilantro
  • 1/2 cup of shredded carrots
  • 1/4 of a cucumber (sliced)
  • 1/4 cup roasted peanuts
  • 1/2 cup of sweet chilli sauce
  • Green seasoning, for cooking the chicken (not in the original notebook, named in step 1 but absent from his list)

Equipment. Instant-read thermometer

How to make it

  1. Cook chicken with green seasoning
  2. Chop the chicken into small pieces
  3. Mix all ingredients together

What goes into it

Two chicken thighs cooked in green seasoning, which is the Trinidadian herb blend rather than anything Thai, and that is what gives this version its own character. Then leaves, a tablespoon of chopped cilantro, half a cup of shredded carrot, a quarter cucumber sliced, and a quarter cup of roasted peanuts.

The green seasoning is named in the first step and nowhere in the ingredient list, so it is promoted into the list above. The dressing is one item: half a cup of bottled sweet chilli sauce.

Is half a cup of dressing too much?

It is double what a published Thai chicken salad uses to dress the same two servings, and that version splits its quarter cup between lime juice, fish sauce, chilli garlic sauce and a little sugar. Here all eight tablespoons are a sugar-based bottled sauce, so the leaves will slump under it if it goes on early.

His quantity stays. If it reads heavy to you, there are two honest ways to fix it: cut the sweet chilli to a quarter cup and let it down with a tablespoon of lime juice and two teaspoons of fish sauce, or leave the half cup alone and call the recipe four servings.

Where this version differs

The method goes straight from chopping the chicken to mixing everything together. Let the chicken cool first, because hot chicken wilts a salad mix in under a minute, and rest it before chopping or the juices run out onto the board.

None of the three Thai pages in the notebook has any acid in it. This salad, the coconut milk shrimp and the chicken marinade all lack lime, which is the thing the category is usually built on. Published versions of this salad also carry thinly sliced red onion. Dress at the table, or serve the sauce alongside, if the bowl is going to sit for any length of time.

Where this version differs

Half a cup of sweet chilli sauce is twice the entire dressing of the reference version for the same two servings, and it carries none of that version's acid or salt. His figure is kept as written; the two ways to bring it into line are set out above.
