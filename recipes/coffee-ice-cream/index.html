Coffee Ice Cream

A French-style custard ice cream flavoured by steeping whole coffee beans in warm milk for an hour rather than by brewing coffee into it, which is why the flavour is round instead of bitter. Five egg yolks to three cups of dairy makes it dense and rich. Half an hour of active work, an hour of steeping, an overnight chill, then churning and a few hours in the freezer to harden. Makes about a litre, eight servings. You need an ice cream maker.

Joseph Bankole30 August 20263 min read

Before you cook this.

The custard reaches 175F to 180F, above the 160F threshold for egg dishes, so the five yolks are cooked and safe. His written method left out the instruction to stir constantly, which is what that safety margin depends on: without it the pan can scramble on the base while reading the right temperature at the surface.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 cups of milk
  • 3/4 cup of sugar
  • 5 egg yolks
  • 1.5 cups of whipping cream
  • 1/4 teaspoon of vanilla
  • 1.5 cups of coffee beans
  • A pinch of salt (not in the original notebook, used in his step 1 but absent from his ingredient list)

Equipment. Ice cream maker, Fine mesh strainer, Instant-read thermometer

Method

  1. Heat the milk, coffee beans, a pinch of salt and 1/2 cup of the cream until steaming but not boiling, about 180F
  2. Take it off the heat, cover, and leave to steep for 1 hour
  3. Put the remaining 1 cup of cream in a bowl and set a fine mesh strainer over it
  4. Whisk the egg yolks and sugar on speed 2 until pale
  5. Reheat the infused milk until it steams again, about 180F
  6. Pour the hot milk slowly into the yolks on speed 2, to temper them
  7. Return everything to the pan and cook over medium heat, stirring constantly and scraping the bottom and corners, until it reaches 175F to 180F and coats the back of a spoon
  8. Pour it through the strainer onto the reserved cream, pressing the beans to get the flavour out, then discard them
  9. Stir in the vanilla
  10. Chill in the fridge overnight
  11. Churn on speed 1 for 15 to 20 minutes, then freeze for 2 to 4 hours to harden

Are five egg yolks safe?

Yes. The custard is cooked. Step 7 takes the yolk mixture to 175F to 180F, comfortably above the 160F at which egg-containing dishes are considered done, so nothing goes into the freezer raw.

What makes that reliable is the stirring. A pan reading 180F at the surface can hold cooler pockets underneath and scrambled egg on the base at the same time, so stir constantly with a spatula and scrape the corners. Use a thermometer rather than guessing.

180F is also the top of the window before a custard curdles. Aim for 175F and use the spoon test as your cue: it should coat the back of a spoon so a finger drawn through leaves a clear path. Cooling it over a bowl of iced water before it goes in the fridge is quicker and gives a smoother texture than sending it straight in hot.

Why the beans come out after the custard is cooked

The notebook page strains the beans early, before the milk is reheated. One strain at the end does three jobs instead of one: it takes out the beans, it catches any egg that scrambled, and it stops the cooking dead by landing the hot custard on cold cream.

That is why a cup of the cream is held back in a bowl with the strainer sitting over it. Press the beans as you strain to get out the flavour they have soaked up.

The notebook page is heavily struck through with duplicate numbering, so the order was reconstructed rather than read straight off. Sugar going in with the yolks instead of with the milk is a genuine variant, not an error, and it changes nothing.
