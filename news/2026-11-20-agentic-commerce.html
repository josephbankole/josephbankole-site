Visa opens Agentic Ready to Stripe, PayPal and Adyen

Joseph Bankole20 November 20262 min read4 sourcesAgentic commerce · Payments

Visa opened its Agentic Ready programme to the acceptance side of the card business on 17 November, launching with more than 140 participants including Adyen, Checkout.com, Fiserv, Global Payments, PayPal and Stripe [1]. Until now the programme was for issuers, and more than 175 of them across 45 markets already use it to test agent-initiated transactions on their own credit and debit cards [1]. The new phase gives merchants, payment service providers, acquirers and commerce platforms a common way to test how a trusted agent’s transaction is recognised, authenticated and processed on the infrastructure they already run [1].

PayPal is working to accept Visa agentic tokens across its merchant network through each merchant’s existing PayPal integration [1]. Adyen lists recognising agent-initiated transactions, accepting network tokens reliably and feeding agentic signals into its fraud tools [1]. Stripe’s Kenny Wolf said the company will test ways to “recognize agent-initiated payments, strengthen merchant controls and improve approval rates” [1]. Closer to home for this desk, Moneris is testing how Canadian merchants can recognise, accept and process the transactions [1]. Visa expects more financial institutions, merchants and enablers to join through 2027 [1].

Mastercard’s agent buys a coffee in Singapore

Mastercard said on 19 November that an AI agent bought a cup of coffee for a consumer from intent the consumer had captured beforehand, with acceptance through Antom, Ant International’s merchant payment platform [2]. The purchase ran on Mastercard Agent Pay with Verifiable Intent, which keeps a verifiable record of what the consumer authorised the agent to do [2]. The release names neither the coffee shop nor the amount [2].

The second milestone was a completed pilot of Agent Pay for Machines with Antom in Singapore, in which a business’s agent used its credential, policy controls and payment authorisation to buy digital services inside limits the business had set [2]. Mastercard introduced the product in June: a business credentials its agents, defines what they may buy and spend, and the spending limits are enforced at the point of payment, with an auditable record of each transaction [2].

DBS opens a bank door to USDC

DBS, Singapore’s largest bank, announced an agreement with Circle on 18 November under which eligible institutional clients will be able to mint and burn USDC directly through the bank, around the clock, with near-instant US dollar settlement [3]. Both firms take part in Project BLOOM, Singapore’s work on making different forms of digital money interoperable [3]. Round-the-clock minting is already normal for stablecoins, so the open item is the cash leg: Ledger Insights asks who provides the dollar funding at the weekend [3].

The World Bank tries USDC for disbursements

The World Bank Group said on 17 November that it will explore, with Citi and Circle, whether regulated US dollar stablecoins could work alongside or improve the correspondent banking arrangements it uses to disburse funds [4]. Citi brings its connections to existing banking rails, and Circle’s infrastructure would mint and settle the funds in USDC [4]. The work builds on FundsChain, a World Bank Group blockchain platform already tracking disbursements on more than 350 projects across 96 countries [4]. The first phase covers borrower countries where digital assets are supported and permitted, and the Group says it will look at bringing in other banks over time [4].
