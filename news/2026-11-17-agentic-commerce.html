OpenAI's ChatGPT Wallet lets AI agents pay at checkout

Joseph Bankole17 November 20262 min read5 sourcesAgentic commerce · Payments

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday 13 November, a payment feature that lets its “dots” agents buy things on a user’s behalf, built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa [1]. It opens first to US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and over [1]. The user adds a card once, asks a dot to find something and approves each purchase. The dot never sees the card. The network hands it a payment token instead: a card number, security code and expiry date scoped to that one transaction [1].

Before checkout the user reviews the items, the total and the card, then approves with Face ID or a fingerprint, which leaves a record of the transaction [2]. Card rewards and the right to dispute a charge carry over [2]. Spending limits and pre-approval by category or time period are still to come [2]. OpenAI is taking no cut of transactions to start, and this is its second go at checkout, after an Instant Checkout feature it quickly pivoted away from [1].

The retailer keeps the checkout, and the fraud rules

The wallet pays through each retailer’s existing checkout instead of holding the sale inside ChatGPT, and the business keeps “control over pricing, fulfillment, returns, and customer service”, OpenAI said in its announcement, as quoted by Digital Commerce 360 [3]. No retailer accepting the wallet has been named [3]. OpenAI concedes that merchants’ own fraud and security systems can decline agent purchases. Its Commerce Partner Playbook asks retailers to recognise signed agent requests through Web Bot Auth at the CDN or firewall, and it names Adyen, Checkout.com and Stripe, with Forter, Riskified, Sardine and Signifyd, as the payment and fraud firms it is working with [3]. For a merchant running agentic commerce acceptance, a decline from its own fraud model now lands on an order the customer has already approved with a fingerprint.

An issuer puts a passkey behind the agent

UQPAY, a Singapore-based payments group that issues Visa cards, said on 17 November that it has put Visa Payment Passkey into production, among the earlier issuers in Asia Pacific to do so [4]. The passkey, built on FIDO standards, lets a cardholder confirm an online card payment with device biometrics or a device passcode instead of a password or a one-time code [4].

The agent link sits in Visa Intelligent Commerce. Once a cardholder adds a card and binds a device, the passkey handles later authentication, so a purchase started by an agentic payment provider traces back to the cardholder’s own approval [4]. UQPAY joined Visa’s Agentic Ready Programme as a principal issuing member in April [4], and the release gives no transaction numbers and names no merchant [4].

Commerce puts an agent on the storefront

Commerce, the company behind BigCommerce, is rolling out Shopping Agent to eligible BigCommerce merchants ahead of the holiday season, ChannelX reported on 16 November [5]. Each merchant customises the assistant, which recommends and compares products, carries context through follow-up questions and guides the shopper to the cart and checkout [5]. It runs on a new conversational storefront search, now on every paid BigCommerce plan, and a separate B2B Agent for negotiated pricing and purchase orders is in early access [5]. More regions and verticals will follow, on no stated date [5].
