Affirm expects AI agents to choose interest-free loans

Joseph Bankole6 November 20263 min read5 sourcesAgentic commerce · Payments

Max Levchin, chief executive of Affirm, told analysts on Thursday 5 November that he expects interest-free instalment volume “to go up a lot in agentic”, because a shopping agent comparing payment options should steer buyers to loans that spread the price without adding interest [1]. Those 0% plans were 28% of Affirm’s gross merchandise volume in the September quarter; interest-bearing loans were the other 72% [1].

The route he described runs through the brand. About 1 million products in the Affirm app carry brand-funded promotions, where a manufacturer pays for the interest-free loan on one item sold through a retailer, and Affirm is building tools that could eventually let a seller’s AI agent make one of those offers to a shopper’s agent [1]. No date came with “eventually”. Anyone building checkout for agentic commerce gets one more field the agent reads and ranks next to price, and it has to be machine-readable before the agent arrives.

Levchin was less tidy on risk. “Generally speaking, you can expect AI commerce to be more than a little bit messy,” he said, pointing to agents buying things consumers did not want and to fraud; he described using data and machine learning to manage it and left open who would bear the liability for fraud involving an agent [1]. The quarter around those remarks: revenue up 35% to $1.3 billion, volume up 34% to $14.4 billion, US balances 30 or more days past due at 2.9% in September, and shares down more than 9% in extended trading [1].

firmly raises $15 million to do the integration once

firmly, a Seattle startup, announced a $15 million Series A on 5 November led by Squarepoint Capital, with J.P. Morgan Payments, Mastercard and Samsung NEXT among the strategic investors [2]. What it sells is the integration bill. firmly Connect plugs into a merchant’s existing commerce stack and presents catalogue and checkout to AI assistants, publishers and social platforms, which the company says reaches more than 11 million merchants representing more than $900 billion in GMV, with each merchant kept as merchant of record [2]. President Damien Balsan called today’s protocol standards “fragmented and can be slow to implement for merchants” [2].

The round came with Ask Agent, a conversational shopper a merchant adds to its own site with a single line of JavaScript; firmly says early deployments lifted conversion by up to 18% and average order value by up to 23% [2]. The release names none of those merchants. An “up to” figure describes the best deployment, so the number worth asking for is the median.

Microsoft lets a shop test whether an agent can check out

Microsoft added three features to Clarity, its website and app analytics tool, in a blog post on Wednesday 4 November [3]. Agentic Analytics shows site owners what AI agents do on their pages and runs simulated assessments of whether those agents can complete a task, aimed at shops losing sales because an agent cannot find a promotion or finish checkout [3]. Brand Agents now help retailers get products surfaced in Copilot, Bing and MSN [3]. Checkout test suites are usually written for a person with a mouse. A simulated agent run can find the broken promotion field before a customer’s agent does.

Pliant raises $80 million for the licensed layer

Pliant, a Berlin business payments company, raised $80 million in a Series C led by Seaya Nova and Speedinvest, announced on 4 November [4]. Its platform covers commercial card issuing, payment accounts, foreign exchange and spend management, and third-party agents can connect through Model Context Protocol to build payment steps into business processes [4]. Pliant says the money will also fund expansion into the United States and Canada [4]. Seaya Nova’s Vinoth Jayakumar gave the investor’s answer to the question Levchin left open: “When agents move money, someone still has to be licensed, auditable and liable, and that is the layer Pliant owns” [4].

Walmart said on 21 October that Agent Profiles, which let a customer approve each agent purchase or set a spending limit per agent, would roll out the following month [5]. That month is November, and Walmart has not named the day.
