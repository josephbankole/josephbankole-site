Amazon shut Meta’s Muse out of Amazon.com, and Shopify put Shop Pay behind it the next morning

Joseph Bankole22 September 20264 min read7 sourcesAgentic commerce · Payments

Amazon has stopped letting Meta’s Muse agent buy anything on Amazon.com. GeekWire’s Todd Bishop published the story at 11:05 pm on Sunday 20 September 2026 and updated it at 10:49 am the next morning, writing that “as of Sunday night, people trying to use Muse to shop on Amazon were seeing the popup” [1]. implicator.ai dates the block to 20 September as well [2].

Amazon’s statement to GeekWire does not mention payments once. “We think it’s fairly straightforward that third-party applications that offer to make purchases on behalf of customers from other businesses should operate openly and respect service provider decisions about whether or not to participate”, it reads, and “Agentic third-party applications such as Muse have the same obligations, and we’ve requested that Meta remove Amazon from the experience” [1]. Asked whether it would take legal action, Amazon declined to comment, and Meta did not immediately respond to GeekWire’s request that Sunday night [1]. Meta’s own line on Muse is about credential handling: the agent “has no visibility into people’s passwords or payment methods”, and credentials “go into secure storage, so Muse can use them without seeing them, including passwords a person types into the browser themselves” [1].

The two statements answer different questions. Meta is describing how the agent holds a card, while Amazon is describing who decides whether the agent is let in at all. Checkout was always the solved half of Muse: the 21 September edition of this brief traced it to Stripe’s Link wallet issuing a single-use virtual card scoped to the approved purchase, good at more than a million businesses that accept Link [3]. A single-use card number has nothing to say to a retailer who declines to be in the basket at all.

What the scale numbers do and do not say

GeekWire puts Amazon’s advertising revenue last year at $68 billion, and notes that Muse was the number one free app on Apple’s United States App Store a week after its 8 September 2026 launch [1]. Axios, carrying the story the same day, reaches for two forecasts: Coveo’s 2026 Commerce Relevance Report puts at 16 per cent the share of shoppers comfortable letting an AI assistant find and buy products for them, and McKinsey’s estimate is up to $1 trillion of United States retail revenue from AI-mediated search and purchase automation by 2030 [4]. The 16 per cent measures stated comfort in a survey and the trillion is a projection four years out. Neither is a count of agent purchases, and nobody has published one of those.

Shopify went the other way inside a day

On the morning of 21 September 2026 Shopify’s chief executive, Tobi Lütke, said the company is “partnering deeply with Muse to enable agentic checkout with Shop Pay on all Shopify stores”. That is quoted from his own post by implicator.ai; the post returns HTTP 402 to a reader without an account, so the carrier is the only version of it available here [2]. American Banker’s John Adams had the Shopify strategy on 18 September and updated the piece on 21 September [5]. The business it bolts onto is substantial. Shopify reported second-quarter gross merchandise volume of $116 billion, up 32 per cent, with Shopify Payments taking 68 per cent of that volume, 75 per cent of Shop Pay customers using it again within twelve months, and Shop Pay’s average order value running about 16 per cent above a non-Shop Pay order [5]. Shopify’s vice-president of product, Rohit Mishra, told American Banker that “agentic commerce really excites us. The agents don’t have the same challenges as humans” [5].

One agent now carries two merchant-side wallets: Link across the Link network, Shop Pay across Shopify’s. The routing question stops being which rail wins and becomes a per-merchant lookup your code has to make before it offers a button. A partnership is not a shipped feature, and neither Lütke nor American Banker gives a date on which a shopper can actually use it [2] [5].

The first calendar entry

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission announced a roundtable series on 21 September 2026, press release 9301-26, and the opening Frontier Forum falls on 28 October 2026 with artificial intelligence and agentic finance as its subject [6]. Chairman Michael S. Selig’s line is that “the technologies transforming our markets are rapidly advancing, and the CFTC must engage directly with the people building, using, and studying them”, and speaker nominations go in by email [6]. PYMNTS carried it the same day [7]. There is no proposed rule in the release and no comment deadline, and payments and checkout are not mentioned anywhere in it. The commission’s remit is derivatives, so agentic finance here does not mean agentic commerce [6].

Nothing else in agentic commerce or payments carried a number worth printing between 20 and 22 September 2026. The Know Your Agent interoperability framework, the Alchemy and Mastercard Agent Pay tie-up and the CFTC’s passive-software-provider guidance all landed earlier in the month and are already in this desk’s archive.

Amazon asked Meta to take Amazon out of Muse on the same weekend Shopify offered Muse every store it has.
